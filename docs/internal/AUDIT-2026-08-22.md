# Audit 2026-08-22 — the design language against Anthropic's

Run against the prompt in `AUDIT-PROMPT-LANGUAGE.md`. Every number below was
measured from the **live DOM** of `claude.ai/code` and `claude.ai/chat/…` in a
logged-in browser, and from the kit's own rendered pages on `localhost:4322`,
in the same session and at the same device pixel ratio. Nothing here is read
off a screenshot or inferred from CSS.

Nothing in `src/`, `docs/` or `tools/` was changed. This is the verdict only.

---

## Verdict

The kit is a faithful reading of the reference in its **surfaces, its colour
discipline and its composition**, and the chat screen assembled this month sits
within a couple of pixels of the real thing on the measurements that matter
most — the composer is 44.9px against 44, the user bubble's film is 6% black
against 5%, its corner is 12 against 12.

The largest divergence is not a value, it is a **missing law**. The reference
sizes a control's corner as **height ÷ 4** and holds it across three different
heights on one screen. The kit's four rungs give 0.200, 0.231, 0.250 and 0.211
— it hits the reference's ratio at exactly one rung and drifts at the other
three, and its own gate permits the drift because it checks a *band* where the
reference has a *constant*.

The second is that the scale-grid theory is **correct and measurable**: the
reference runs four control heights in a single view, and they are siblings,
not density regions. The kit has one component that can do this.

---

## The scale grid — verdict on the theory

**Confirmed.** On one screen (`/code`, no scrolling, one density) the reference
renders:

| Height | Radius | Ratio | Type | Padding-x | What wears it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **20** | 5 | **0.250** | 12px | 6 / 4 / 0 | `Auto`, `Sonnet 5`, add, mic, dictation |
| **24** | 6 | **0.250** | 13px | 6 / 4 / 0 | send, filter, `Default`, file rows |
| 24 | 8 | 0.333 | 13px | 4 | sidebar section headers |
| 30 | 6 | 0.200 | 14px | 4 | the `Home`/`Code` segmented pair |
| **32** | 8 | **0.250** | 14px | 2 / 0 | collapse, search, account row |

Four heights, three type sizes, three radii — **in one view, at one density,
among siblings**. A `20px` control and a `32px` control sit in the same
sidebar. No density attribute can express that; only a per-instance rung can.

You were right to push back on my earlier framing: the two mechanisms do not
compete. The button already carries four rungs *and* retunes with density —
`--control-h-sm` is 26 at default and 30 at comfortable. They multiply. The
question was never which wins; it is only **which components need a rung**.

### What the kit has

Nine class families carry a size modifier, and they disagree about the rungs:

| Family | Rungs |
|---|---|
| `inst-btn` | xs · sm · (md) · lg |
| `inst-input` `inst-textarea` `inst-select` | sm · (md) · lg |
| `inst-ring` | xs · sm · (lg) |
| `inst-history` `inst-avatar` | sm · (md) · lg |
| `inst-icon` | sm only |
| `inst-share` | lg only |

Sixty-four components have no rung. On the evidence above, that is **not** a
sixty-four-item backlog. The reference varies exactly three kinds of thing by
size — a button, an icon, and a row — and the kit already ladders the first two.

### What the ladders should be

The comparison, kit against reference, default density:

| Rung | Kit h | Ref h | Kit r | Ref r | Kit ratio | Ref ratio | Kit type | Ref type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| xs | **20** | **20** ✓ | 4 | **5** | 0.200 | 0.250 | 11 | **12** |
| sm | **26** | **24** | 6 | 6 | 0.231 | 0.250 | 12.5 | **13** |
| md | **32** | **32** ✓ | 8 | 8 ✓ | **0.250** ✓ | 0.250 | 14 | **14** ✓ |
| lg | 38 | — | 8 | — | 0.211 | — | 16 | — |

The ends agree and the middle does not. `xs` and `md` land exactly on the
reference; `sm` is two pixels tall and half a point small; `lg` has no
counterpart at all — the reference's largest control on either screen is 32.

### Recommendations, ranked

1. **Adopt `radius = height ÷ 4` as a law**, and let `cmd/proportion` check an
   equality rather than a band. The band exists because the ratios drifted; it
   was fitted to the drift. This is case 3 from the prompt — a divergence with
   no reason written anywhere.
2. **Move `sm` from 26 to 24.** It is the most-used rung after `md` and the only
   one that misses a reference height the kit otherwise matches.
3. **Do not add rungs to the sixty-four.** Nothing in the reference asks for a
   panel, a card, a table or a step at two sizes on one screen. A ladder nobody
   needs is API weight, and two classes were deleted this month for being that.
4. **Fill `inst-icon`'s ladder before anything else.** It has one rung (`sm`) and
   the reference uses at least two glyph sizes; the kit's `--icon-size` variable
   already does the work, so this is naming, not mechanism.
5. **Re-examine `lg`.** It may be the kit's own requirement (touch, large scale)
   rather than drift — but the reason is not written down, which makes it a
   finding regardless of the answer.

## Divergences, ranked

**P0 — the corner has no law.**
Ref: 20→5, 24→6, 32→8, all exactly ÷4. Kit: 0.200 / 0.231 / 0.250 / 0.211.
*Shown on:* every control on both screens.
*Which is right:* the reference. A single ratio is checkable by equality; a band
is a hole a drift fits inside.
*Cost:* two token values and one gate rule.
*Case:* 3 — deliberate-looking, undocumented.

**P0 — the smallest rung's corner and glyph.**
Ref `xs`: 20px box, 5px corner, **12px glyph** (0.60 of the box).
Kit `xs`: 20px box, 4px corner, **14px glyph** (0.70).
*Shown on:* the composer session row, both sides.
*Cost:* one value each. The glyph change touches `--size-icon-sm`, which is
shared with the status bar — check that first.
*Case:* 1 — the kit is behind.

**P1 — the agent's answer is a size small and a step loose.**
Ref: **16px / 24px leading (1.50)**. Kit: 14px / 1.65.
*Shown on:* `font-claude-response-body` against `.inst-turn-body`.
*Which is right:* the reference, and by its own logic — a long answer is the
thing being read, and the kit sets it smaller than the reference sets its
*sidebar*. The kit's 1.65 is `--leading-prose`, chosen for prose; the reference
runs 1.5 at a larger size, which is more air in absolute terms and less in
relative.
*Case:* 1.

**P1 — the user's message is set as prose and is not prose.**
Ref: 15px / 20px leading (**1.33**). Kit: 14px / **1.65**.
*Shown on:* `[data-testid="user-message"]` against `.inst-turn[data-from="user"]`.
A one-line instruction at 1.65 reads as a paragraph with nothing in it. The kit
applies its prose leading to both halves of the exchange; the reference splits
them — tight for the ask, loose for the answer.
*Case:* 3 — the kit has a rule (`--leading-prose` is "the body of an agent's
answer") and does not follow it on the user's side.

**P2 — the user bubble's horizontal padding.**
Ref 12 / **16**. Kit **12 / 12**. The film (6% vs 5%) and the corner (12 vs 12)
already match; only the inset differs.

**P2 — the composer's corner.** Ref 10, kit 12. Heights agree (44 vs 44.9).

**P2 — chrome weight, and the kit is close but not equal.**
The reference's whole `/code` screen carries **three** filled or bordered boxes:
the segmented track, its selected segment, and the composer. Everything else is
type on the page ground. This is the axis where the kit historically drifted
and it has visibly improved; it is worth one deliberate pass counting boxes on
an assembled kit screen against three.

## What NOT to change

- **The switch.** The reference's off track is a pale capsule that fails WCAG
  1.4.11; `--switch-track` at 3:1 is the kit being right. Already settled this
  month, and the measurement stands.
- **The focus ring.** The reference's is lighter than the kit's *was*; the kit
  now matches its weight while keeping a 3:1 separation the reference does not
  attempt. Case 2, reason written down.
- **Surfaces and depth.** `--surface-page` 0.976 against a raised 1.0 is the
  same *direction* and nearly the same *step* as the reference's 252/252/251
  page against white cards. No divergence found.
- **The user bubble's film.** 6% against 5% is below the threshold of a visible
  difference on any theme measured.
- **The four text tiers.** The reference shows three on these screens, but its
  screens have no tone vocabulary — no ok/warn/error readouts. Not comparable
  from this evidence; do not treat the kit's extra tiers as bloat on this basis.

---

## Undocumented decisions found

1. **Why the control ratios differ per rung.** No comment anywhere derives
   0.200 / 0.231 / 0.211. The band in `cmd/proportion` documents the *range*,
   not the choice.
2. **Why `--control-h-lg` is 38.** It is the one rung with no reference
   counterpart and no stated derivation.
3. **Why `--leading-prose` is applied to a user's turn.** `.inst-prose` is
   documented as "the body of an agent's answer"; `.inst-turn-body` takes the
   same leading on both sides without saying why.

---

## Method note

The reference was measured, not eyeballed: computed styles pulled from the live
DOM, tallied by shape, and cross-tabulated height × radius × type so the ladder
fell out of the data rather than out of a guess. Screenshot compositing was
unavailable in this session, which turned out not to matter — every claim above
is a number, and a number from `getComputedStyle` is better evidence than a
number counted off a raster.
